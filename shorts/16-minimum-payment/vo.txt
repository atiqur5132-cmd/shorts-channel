Your credit card's minimum payment is built to keep you paying for years.
Say you owe five thousand dollars at twenty-two percent, the Fed's average for cards that charge interest.
The minimum is often one percent of the balance, plus interest. About one hundred forty dollars.
But over ninety of that is just interest.
Pay only the minimum, and it takes over sixteen years. Interest: about seven thousand seven hundred dollars.
Pay two hundred fifty a month instead? Done in about two years, under thirteen hundred in interest.
So check the minimum payment box on your statement. It shows the real cost.
